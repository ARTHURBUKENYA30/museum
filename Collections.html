COLLECTIONS

Sixteen collection areas reflect how a national printing and publishing industry works — from presses and type to ink, offset, security print, paper, binding and the documents that reached the public. Open any collection for a dedicated page focused only on that subject.

1. Printing Presses & Machinery

Letterpress, offset and finishing equipment used in government and commercial production. Includes presses, feeders, folders and related workshop tools that show how ink met paper at industrial scale.

Core object collection

2. Type, Composition & Typography

Foundry type, type cases, matrices, composing sticks and specimen sheets. This collection documents the hand and machine composition skills that preceded digital pre-press.

Craft & design focus

3. Official Publications & Gazettes

Government gazettes, statutory instruments, forms and official stationery. These publications are primary sources for law, administration and the visual language of the state.

Primary research collection

4. Newspapers & Periodicals

Selected runs of national and regional newspapers and serials linked to UPPC production or distribution. They show how daily and weekly print shaped public debate.

Periodical holdings

5. Books, Textbooks & Bound Works

Educational and official books produced or finished in the national print system — from school texts to bound volumes that demonstrate edition structure and binding practice.

Publishing heritage

6. Bookbinding & Finishing

Tools and samples from binding, stitching, trimming and finishing — the stages that turn printed sheets into durable books, registers and official volumes.

Finishing trades

7. Photographic & Visual Record

Photographs of print works, staff, distribution and public communication campaigns. These images document labour, plant and the everyday life of the industry.

Visual documentation

8. Oral Histories & Trade Knowledge

Recorded accounts from compositors, press operators, binders, administrators and readers — preserving skills and memories that objects alone cannot explain.

Living memory

9. Pre-press & Design

Layouts, paste-up methods, film and plate-making materials that bridge composition and the press — the design and preparation stages of print production.

Design & process

10. Paper & Materials

Paper stocks, boards and related materials used for official forms, books and newspapers — showing how substrate choices affected durability and appearance.

Materials science of print

11. Ephemera & Public Notices

Posters, handbills, tickets and short-lived notices that carried urgent public information — often the most fragile and revealing printed artefacts.

Everyday print culture

12. Offset & Lithography

Plates, blankets, offset units and process samples that document the shift from relief letterpress to planographic offset production in official and commercial work.

Process technology

13. Ink, Colour & Chemistry

Ink samples, colour standards, mixing records and related materials that controlled density, drying and appearance on official and commercial sheets.

Colour & materials

14. Security & Specialised Print

Certificates, controlled forms, numbered stationery and other specialised products that required tighter process control than ordinary public print.

Controlled production

15. Commercial Stationery & Jobbing

Letterheads, invoices, tickets and short-run commercial work that sat alongside government production in many print houses.

Jobbing print

Gauges, quoins, furniture, measuring tools and general workshop equipment that supported accurate make-ready and safe operation of the press room.

Printing Presses & Machinery

This collection holds the industrial heart of national printing: presses and related equipment that carried official notices, newspapers and books into public circulation. Objects range from letterpress frames to offset units and finishing machines.

Historic press line

Older printing machines of the kind used for official forms, notices and short-run government work.

These machines show how national print capacity was organised before digital workflows. Visitors can compare frame design, inking systems and sheet handling with later industrial presses in the same gallery.

Industrial press & rollers

Ink rollers and press units that transferred image and text onto paper at production scale.

Roller condition, ink distribution and pressure settings determined print quality. The museum interprets these mechanical choices alongside surviving printed sheets so process and product can be studied together.

Wooden / early press

Earlier press forms that illustrate the long path from hand-powered printing to mechanised production.

Early presses help explain why official printing was once slow and specialised, and why the expansion of mechanised capacity in the twentieth century transformed public communication in Uganda.

Flatbed & factory presses

Heavy production presses associated with high-volume official and commercial printing.

Factory-scale equipment supported gazettes, textbooks and newspapers. Interpretive labels link each machine type to typical products held elsewhere in the collections.

Type, Composition & Typography

Before digital pre-press, every line of public text was set by hand or machine. This collection preserves type metal, cases, matrices and specimen material that record the craft of composition in East African printing houses.

Metal letterpress type

Individual metal characters used to set headlines and body text before digital composition.

Each character is a raised relief surface. Compositors selected and arranged them by hand, a skill that defined the speed and appearance of official print for generations.

Type blocks & cases

Sorted type and blocks stored for repeated use in the composing room.

Type cases organised alphabets for rapid access. The museum preserves case layouts and samples so visitors can see how a composing room was structured in practice.

Composing stick

The tool used to assemble lines of metal type by hand before locking them into a forme.

Lines were set to a fixed measure, justified with spacing material, then transferred to a galley. This tool is central to understanding pre-digital composition.

From type to page

How composed letterforms became the printed page citizens read in gazettes and books.

Once locked in a forme and inked, type transferred its image under pressure. Surviving pages in the museum show the results of these choices in public documents.

Oral Histories & Trade Knowledge

Recorded accounts from compositors, press operators, binders, administrators and readers preserve skills and workplace memory that objects and documents alone cannot fully explain.

Readers & administrators

Access for research

Selected recordings are available for supervised research use; enquire via Research Access.

Research Access

Scholars, students and independent researchers may request access to designated collections by appointment. Supervised reading is available for gazettes, selected newspapers, photographic materials and other holdings approved for research use.

When you write to us, please include a short description of your research topic, the types of material you hope to consult, preferred dates, and your institutional affiliation if any. This helps the curatorial team prepare relevant materials and advise on access conditions.

Handling rules protect fragile items for future users. Photography and reproduction requests are considered case by case in line with institutional policy and the condition of the material.

Design tools

Instruments and materials used in preparing artwork for print.

From rules and knives to film work, these tools explain intermediate stages between writing and the press.

Plate & press readiness

How prepared forms and plates reached the machine room.

Pre-press quality determined registration, clarity and waste rates on the production floor.

From design to document

Examples linking design decisions to official and educational print.

Collection 10

Paper & Materials

Paper stocks and related materials shaped the durability, cost and appearance of official forms, books and newspapers.

Paper stocks

Grades used for forms, books and serial publications.

Weight, finish and fibre content affected how well documents survived handling and storage.

Boards & covers

Materials used for covers, registers and durable volumes.

Board quality often determined whether a volume remained usable after decades of institutional use.

Print on paper

How ink behaviour changes across different stocks.

Comparative samples help explain show-through, absorbency and archival risk in historical print.

Storage & survival

Why some papers last and others fail.

Environmental history and material choice are central to conservation planning in the museum.

Offset & Lithography

This page focuses solely on offset and lithographic process: plates, blankets, unit design and sample sheets that mark the industry’s move from relief letterpress toward planographic production.

Offset units

Cylinder and unit arrangements typical of sheet-fed offset production.

Offset separates image carrier from paper via a blanket cylinder, allowing finer control on many paper stocks used for official and commercial work.

Plant context

How offset equipment sat alongside earlier presses in mixed production houses.

Many national printers ran letterpress and offset in parallel during transition decades; this collection interprets that coexistence.

Process samples

Sheets that show registration, ink lay and finishing after offset printing.

Comparing samples with letterpress output helps visitors see why institutions adopted offset for volume and quality.

Plates & prep

Links to pre-press materials used to prepare offset plates.

Plate-making quality determined whether a job stayed in register across a full run.

Ink, Colour & Chemistry

Dedicated to ink and colour: density, drying, mixing and the standards that made official pages legible and consistent. This page does not mix in presses or bound books — only colour and ink-related holdings.

Ink samples

Examples of process and spot inks used on institutional work.

Ink behaviour on different papers affected show-through, drying time and archival permanence.

Colour standards

Reference practices used to keep institutional colour consistent across jobs.

Even monochrome official print depended on reliable black density and clean impression.

On-paper results

How ink choices appeared once dry on production stocks.

Surviving sheets are compared with process notes where those records still exist.

Ink & paper

Interaction between ink formulation and paper absorbency.

See also the Paper & Materials collection for substrate-focused holdings.

Tickets & small forms

Everyday print culture
