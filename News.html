12 October 2026 · Events

Curator’s Talk: Ink & Independence

The museum invites the public to a curator-led talk on Ink & Independence, the current exhibition exploring how gazettes, newspapers and public notices recorded Uganda’s transition to independence and the early years of the Republic.

The session will walk through selected objects on display in Gallery One, including official publications and press-related material that show how print supported political change and everyday administration.

Date: Saturday 8 November 2026
Time: 11:00
Place: Gallery One, UPPC Print Heritage Museum
Admission: Free; registration preferred

To register, email museum@uppc.co.ug or use the contact form. School groups should book through the Programs team.

/ / Research access

28 September 2026 · Research

Research appointments open for Q4

The curatorial team is accepting research access requests for October–December 2026. Scholars, postgraduate students and independent researchers may request supervised consultation of designated gazettes, newspapers, photographic materials and other holdings approved for research use.

When you write, please include: a short description of your research topic; the types of material you hope to consult; preferred dates; and your institutional affiliation if any. This helps staff prepare relevant materials and advise on access conditions.

Handling rules protect fragile items for future users. Photography and reproduction requests are considered case by case according to institutional policy and the condition of the material.

Email museum@uppc.co.ug with the subject line “Research Access Request”.

/ / Oral histories

5 August 2026 · Collections

New oral history recordings accessioned

Three new interviews with former UPPC press operators have been accessioned into the Oral Histories & Trade Knowledge collection. The recordings document press-room routines, training pathways and the transition from letterpress practices toward newer production methods.

Oral history complements machinery and printed documents by capturing skills and workplace memory that objects alone cannot fully explain. The new material will support exhibition interpretation and supervised research use.

Researchers interested in consulting these recordings should follow the standard research access process and note “oral history” in their request.
