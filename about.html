UPPC Print Heritage Museum

Preserving Uganda's Printed History Since 1902

Welcome to the UPPC Print Heritage Museum in Entebbe — a place dedicated to preserving, interpreting and celebrating the machines, craftsmanship, skills and stories behind Uganda's printed history.

From the wooden press of 1902 to modern digital printing, the museum tells the story of how ink and paper helped build a nation.

Our Vision

A Living Centre of Print Heritage

To be Uganda's leading centre for preserving and celebrating the art, science and heritage of printing — from the 1902 wooden nipping press that printed the first Protectorate Gazettes, to the Heidelberg SORD that printed Independence, to the digital future.

Our Mission

Preserving the Machines, Skills and Stories

The UPPC Print Heritage Museum exists to collect, restore, document and showcase the machines, skills and stories that built Uganda Printing and Publishing Corporation and the printed history of Uganda.

01

Preserve

Preserve our oldest treasures as working heritage for future generations.

1902 Wooden Press · 1955 SOAG Stitcher · 1959 Halley Randall · 1968 Luke & Spencer Pacer-36 · Wrigley Forklift · Polar · SORD
02

Educate

Educate students, printers and the public on how print shaped governance, education and independence in Uganda.

From hand-set type and roll-fed numbering to offset printing.
03

Inspire

Inspire pride in Ugandan craftsmanship, innovation and service by keeping our machines working, our stories told and our heritage alive.

Heritage for today's printers and tomorrow's innovators.

The Museum Story

From Ink on Paper to a Nation in Print

Welcome to UPPC Print Heritage Museum

Est. 1902 — Entebbe

Before computers, before offset, before Uganda was Uganda — there was ink, paper and a wooden press.

This museum holds the machines that printed:

  • The Uganda Protectorate Gazettes (1902)
  • The Legislative Council Hansards (1950s)
  • The Independence Constitution (1962)
  • Government Gazettes, school books, licences and receipts produced over more than a century.

From hand-press to Speedmaster — We Printed a Nation.
