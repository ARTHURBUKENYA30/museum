UPPC Print Heritage Museum · Entebbe

Preserving Uganda's Printed History Since 1902

“Where Every Page of Uganda Was Pressed.”

The UPPC Print Heritage Museum preserves the machines, skills, publications and stories that shaped Uganda's printing and publishing history — from the wooden presses of the early twentieth century to modern digital technologies.

Our Purpose

Our Mission

The UPPC Print Heritage Museum exists to collect, restore, document and showcase the machines, skills and stories that built Uganda Printing and Publishing Corporation and the printed history of Uganda.

P

Preserve

Our oldest treasures — including the 1902 Wooden Press, 1955 SOAG Stitcher, 1959 Halley Randall, 1968 Luke & Spencer Pacer-36 No. M12771, Wrigley Forklift, Polar, SORD and modern presses — as working heritage for future generations.

E

Educate

Students, printers and the public on how print shaped governance, education and independence in Uganda — from hand-set type to roll-fed numbering and offset printing.

I

Inspire

Pride in Ugandan craftsmanship, innovation and service by keeping our machines working, our stories told and our heritage alive.

Looking Ahead

Our Vision

To be Uganda's leading centre for preserving and celebrating the art, science and heritage of printing — from the 1902 wooden nipping press associated with the early Protectorate Gazettes, through the presses of the independence era, to the digital future.

What We Stand For

Our Core Values

Five values guide how we preserve, interpret and share Uganda's printing heritage.

H

Heritage

Protecting the machines, publications and stories that document Uganda's printed history.

C

Craftsmanship

Honouring the technical skill, knowledge and dedication of generations of printers.

S

Service

Recognising the role of printing in government, education, communication and national development.

I

Innovation

Celebrating the evolution from hand presses to offset and modern digital technologies.

IN

Integrity

Preserving Uganda's print heritage accurately, responsibly and for future generations.

What Was Printed?

Printing became an essential part of Uganda's administrative, educational, legislative and national history.

1902

Uganda Protectorate Gazettes

Official publications associated with the administration of the Uganda Protectorate and the early development of government printing.

1950

Legislative Council Hansards

Printed records of legislative proceedings and debates that form part of Uganda's documentary and parliamentary history.

1962

Independence Constitution

The printed constitutional record associated with Uganda's transition to independence in 1962.

120+

Government & Public Documents

Gazettes, school books, licences, receipts and other printed materials form part of Uganda's long print heritage.

Historical Timeline of Printing & Publishing in Uganda

From the early government press to modern digital publishing, Uganda's printed history reflects more than a century of technological and national development.

The Beginning of Uganda's Government Printing Heritage

Educational Printing

Government Printing Expands

Institutional Growth
