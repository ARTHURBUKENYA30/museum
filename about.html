About the Museum

UPPC Print Heritage Museum - Entebbe

Preserving Uganda's Printed History Since 1902

The UPPC Print Heritage Museum preserves and celebrates the machines, skills, people and stories that built Uganda Printing and Publishing Corporation and shaped Uganda's printed history.

"Where Every Page of Uganda Was Pressed."

Mission

Our Mission

The UPPC Print Heritage Museum exists to collect, restore, document and showcase the machines, skills and stories that built Uganda Printing and Publishing Corporation and the printed history of Uganda.

We preserve our oldest treasures, educate students, printers and the public about how print shaped governance, education and independence, and inspire pride in Ugandan craftsmanship, innovation and service.

Short Mission: To preserve the machines and memories that printed Uganda since 1902, and inspire the printers of tomorrow.

Vision

Our Vision

To be Uganda's leading centre for preserving and celebrating the art, science and heritage of printing - from the 1902 wooden nipping press that printed the first Protectorate Gazettes, to the Heidelberg SORD that printed Independence, to the digital future.

Short Vision: To keep the story of Uganda's print heritage alive, pressed, and working for generations.

National History

Historical Timeline of Printing & Publishing in Uganda

1894

Uganda declared a British Protectorate

Printing was introduced mainly for administrative purposes.

Colonial Foundations
1902

Beginning of Uganda's Print Heritage

The museum traces its print heritage to the 1902 wooden nipping press, associated with the printing of the first Uganda Protectorate Gazettes.

Museum Heritage
1903

Establishment of the Government Printer

The Government Printer became the official printer for government gazettes, laws and notices and was later formalized as UPPC.

Colonial Foundations
1920s–1950s

Missionary presses

Missionary presses produced religious texts, educational materials and early vernacular publications.

Colonial Foundations
1950s

Legislative Council Hansards

Printed Hansards documented proceedings of the Legislative Council, contributing to Uganda's institutional and political record.

Institutional Heritage
1954

Hydroelectric power at Owen Falls Dam

Hydroelectric power boosted industrial capacity, including printing.

Industrial Development
1962

Independence & Institutional Growth

Uganda gained independence, while government printing continued to play an important role in national administration and communication.

Independence
1960s–1970s

Expansion of educational publishing

Educational publishing, newspapers and political materials expanded as printing became increasingly important to national life.

Institutional Growth
1970s

Government printing and state communication

UPPC and government printing operations played an important role in producing official decrees, notices and state communication.

Institutional Growth
1980s–1990s

Growth of private printing firms

Private printing firms expanded, especially around Nasser Road in Kampala.

Liberalization
1992

UPPC Act reaffirmed UPPC

UPPC's role as official government printer was reaffirmed, including responsibility for sensitive documents.

Liberalization
1990s

Modern offset presses

Modern offset presses and limited digital printing technologies were introduced.

Liberalization
2000s

Rise of digital publishing

Digital publishing, photocopy bureaus and small-scale presses grew rapidly.

Modern Era
2010s

UPPC rebranding and modernization

UPPC began rebranding and modernization efforts while navigating funding and equipment challenges.
