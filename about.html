About the Museum

Preserving Uganda's Print Heritage

The About page brings together the museum's mission, vision, historical timeline and institutional leadership.

Mission

Our Mission

To preserve, document, and exhibit the history and evolution of printing and publishing in Uganda, providing an engaging cultural and educational experience that attracts local and international visitors, supports heritage tourism, and promotes national identity.

Vision

Our Vision

A premier national heritage center showcasing Uganda’s rich printing and publishing history, inspiring learning, innovation, and cultural pride, and contributing to Uganda’s tourism growth.

National History

Historical Timeline of Printing & Publishing in Uganda

1894

Uganda declared a British Protectorate

Printing introduced mainly for administrative purposes.

Colonial Foundations
1903

Establishment of the Government Printer

Establishment of the Government Printer, later formalized as UPPC. It became the official printer for government gazettes, laws and notices.

Colonial Foundations
1920s–1950s

Missionary presses

Missionary presses produced religious texts, educational materials and early vernacular publications.

Colonial Foundations
1954

Hydroelectric power at Owen Falls Dam

Hydroelectric power boosted industrial capacity, including printing.

Colonial Foundations
1962

Independence & Institutional Growth

Uganda gains independence; UPPC continues as the official government printer.

Independence
1960s–1970s

Expansion of educational publishing

Expansion of educational publishing, newspapers and political materials.

Institutional Growth
1970s

Idi Amin’s regime

The regime relied heavily on UPPC for official decrees and state communication.

Institutional Growth
1980s–1990s

Growth of private printing firms

Private printing firms expanded, especially around Nasser Road in Kampala.

Liberalization
1992

UPPC Act reaffirmed UPPC

UPPC's role as official government printer was reaffirmed, including responsibility for sensitive documents.

Liberalization
1990s

Modern offset presses

Introduction of modern offset presses and limited digital printing.

Liberalization
2000s

Rise of digital publishing

Digital publishing, photocopy bureaus and small-scale presses grew rapidly.

Modern Era
2010s

UPPC rebranding and modernization

UPPC begins rebranding and modernization efforts while navigating funding and equipment challenges.
