Historical Timeline of Printing & Publishing in Uganda

1902

Colonial Foundations

Government Printer Established in Entebbe

The Government Printer was established in Entebbe in 1902, marking the beginning of Uganda's government printing service. The printing office served the Uganda Protectorate and produced official government materials including gazettes, notices and forms.

1933

Colonial Foundations

S. Foote, Government Printer

In 1933, S. Foote served as Government Printer. His period of leadership focused on official printing services and the production of government publications.

1930s–1940s

Colonial Foundations

J.H. Bowkett — Government Printer

During the 1930s–1940s, J.H. Bowkett served as Government Printer. The period included increased printing output and mechanical improvements to the pressroom, particularly in support of colonial administration.

1930s–1940s

Colonial Foundations

Mechanical Flatbed Printing

Mechanical flatbed printing technology was used during the 1930s–1940s. The technology represented an important stage in the transition from manually operated printing to more mechanised production.

1950s

Independence

Introduction of Monotype Technology

Monotype hot-metal typesetting technology was introduced to improve typography and increase the efficiency of producing government reports, laws and educational materials.

1950s

Independence

K.G. Chard — Monotype Operator

K.G. Chard is identified in the museum timeline as a Monotype Operator during the 1950s. The period reflects the growing use of hot-metal typesetting in government printing.

1962

Institutional Growth

Independence and the Government Printer

At Uganda's independence in 1962, the Government Printer continued under the Office of the President. Printing remained important for official documents, government publications and materials supporting the new nation.

1992

Institutional Growth

Establishment of Uganda Printing & Publishing Corporation Act

The Uganda Printing and Publishing Corporation (UPPC) was formally established as a public enterprise under the UPPC Act of 1992. The Act established UPPC as a statutory corporation and formalised its role in government printing and publishing.

2014

Liberalization

Irene Muwanguzi — Managing Director

Irene Muwanguzi is identified in the timeline as Managing Director in 2014, during a period of modernisation and the introduction of digital workflows.

2019

Liberalization

Prof. Tom Davis — Managing Director

Prof. Tom Davis is identified in the timeline as Managing Director in 2019, with a focus on restructuring, capacity building and strengthening publishing operations.

2021–2023

Liberalization

Mr. Keneth Oluka — Acting Director

Mr. Keneth Oluka is identified as Acting Director in 2021–2023.

2023–Present

Modern Era

Prof. Sudi Nangoli — Managing Director

Prof. Sudi Nangoli became Managing Director in 2023. His tenure is associated with continued modernisation, digital transformation and expansion of printing and publishing services.

Our purpose

Our Mission

The UPPC Print Heritage Museum exists to collect, restore, document and showcase the machines, skills and stories that built Uganda Printing and Publishing Corporation and the printed history of Uganda.

Our Vision

To be Uganda's leading centre for preserving and celebrating the art, science and heritage of printing - from the 1902 wooden nipping press that printed the first Protectorate Gazettes, to the Heidelberg SORD that printed Independence, to the digital future.
